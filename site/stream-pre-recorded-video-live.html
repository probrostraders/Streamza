What is pre-recorded live streaming?

Pre-recorded live streaming (sometimes called simulated live, scheduled live or "fake live") is when a video file that was recorded earlier is pushed to a platform's live endpoint instead of a camera feed. On the viewer's side it is a genuine live stream: it carries the LIVE badge, live chat is open, notifications fire, and the broadcast appears in the platform's live sections. The only difference is that the pixels come from a file rather than from a lens pointed at you right now.

Technically, nothing about the delivery is fake. Your video is read frame by frame and sent over RTMP in real time, at 1× speed, exactly the way an encoder sends a camera feed. The destination platform cannot tell, and does not need to, whether the frames came from a sensor or a hard drive.

Why stream a recording instead of going live for real

  • You get to edit. Cut the mistakes, colour-grade, add captions and lower-thirds, then broadcast the polished cut. Live-to-camera gives you one take.
  • You can be somewhere else. The stream runs on our servers, so you can be asleep, on a plane, or answering chat messages while it plays.
  • Nothing depends on your internet. Once the file is uploaded, a dropped Wi-Fi connection at your end cannot kill the broadcast.
  • You can run around the clock. Loop one file and the channel never goes dark. See the 24/7 live stream guide.
  • Live gets more reach than an upload. Live broadcasts trigger subscriber notifications and surface in live-specific placements that a normal video upload does not.
  • No hardware requirement. No capture card, no gaming PC, no phone left plugged in and overheating.

How to stream a pre-recorded video live

  1. Get your stream key from the platform

    Open the live dashboard of wherever you want to broadcast (YouTube Studio, Twitch Creator Dashboard, Facebook Live Producer, Kick Settings) and copy the RTMP server URL and the stream key. Our stream key guide has the exact click path for every platform.

  2. Open Streamza Web Studio and claim a slot

    Go to streamza.live/studio. There are 10 concurrent live slots; tap a free one. No account and no card are needed to start.

  3. Upload your video and paste the key

    Drop in an MP4 (or MOV, MKV, FLV, up to 1.5 GB). Paste the RTMP URL and stream key. For YouTube, Twitch and Facebook the server URL is filled in for you, so the key is all you need.

  4. Choose loop, then go live

    Switch on loop forever if you want the video to repeat continuously, then hit go. Once the stream is running you can close the tab. It keeps broadcasting from our servers.

Start the stream on the platform too. Most platforms wait for incoming video before they will let you click "Go live". The usual order is: create the live event on the platform, copy the key, start sending from Streamza, wait for the platform's preview to show your video, then confirm the broadcast on the platform.

A premiere is YouTube's own built-in version of this idea, and it is a good option if YouTube is your only destination and you only want to play the video once. Pre-recorded live streaming is what you want when you need looping, other platforms, several platforms at once, or full control over the RTMP feed.

Is streaming pre-recorded video allowed?

Yes. Broadcasting your own recorded content over RTMP is a normal, supported use of every major platform's live infrastructure. Two rules matter, though, and they are worth getting right:

  • It has to be your content. Re-streaming films, TV, sports, other creators' videos or licensed music will get you copyright-struck and, on repeat, banned. Our Acceptable Use Policy prohibits it, and platform detection systems catch it quickly.
  • Do not mislead viewers. Platforms have rules about presenting a recording as something happening right now. Twitch, for instance, expects reruns to be labelled as such. Playing your recorded content live is fine; pretending to interact live when you cannot is not.

Beyond that, individual platforms have their own quirks (Facebook caps continuous broadcasts, Twitch has bitrate ceilings) and each platform guide above covers them.

Quality: what actually gets sent

Streamza relays your file to the destination without re-encoding it wherever possible. That means the resolution, frame rate and bitrate your video already has are what your viewers get: a 1080p60 export arrives as 1080p60, with no generational quality loss from a second encode, and no CPU-bound frame drops of the kind you get when a laptop tries to encode and stream at once.

The practical implication: quality is decided in your editor, not at broadcast time. Export at the resolution and bitrate your destination recommends, and that is exactly what goes out.

Frequently asked questions

Will viewers know the video is pre-recorded?

Not from the stream itself. It carries the live badge, live chat and live notifications exactly like a camera broadcast. What gives it away is behaviour: if you cannot answer chat in real time, regular viewers will work it out, so it is best to be upfront about it.

Do I need OBS or any other software?

No. Streamza does the encoding and the RTMP push on its own servers. There is nothing to download or install. See streaming without OBS.

Can I turn my computer off while it streams?

Yes, that is the point of it running in the cloud. Once the stream has started you can close the browser tab, shut the laptop or power the phone down, and the broadcast continues.

How long can the stream run?

Claiming a slot gives you a free 15-minute trial. To keep broadcasting, the slot converts to a paid 24-hour slot which you can renew for as long as you need, including indefinitely, for an always-on channel.

What video formats can I upload?

MP4, MOV, MKV and FLV up to 1.5 GB. H.264 video with AAC audio is the most reliable combination because it passes straight through to the platform without re-encoding.

Can I stream to more than one platform at the same time?

Yes, up to three destinations simultaneously on the Multistream plan: one upload, three live streams. See multistreaming.

Can I loop the video so the channel never goes offline?

Yes. Turn on "loop forever" before starting and the file replays continuously with no gap between iterations. The 24/7 guide covers how to pick a video that loops cleanly.

Does it cost anything to try?

No. Every slot claim starts with a free 15-minute trial, no account, no card, no email required.
