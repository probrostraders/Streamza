Why people look for an OBS alternative

Searches for "live stream without OBS" almost always come down to one of five walls:

  • The computer cannot handle it. Encoding 1080p in software while the machine does anything else means dropped frames, and older laptops simply cannot do it at all.
  • There is no computer. Plenty of people work from a phone or a tablet, where OBS does not exist.
  • The upload connection is too slow or unstable. Live encoding needs sustained bandwidth for the entire broadcast; one hiccup and the stream stutters.
  • It has to run for days. Nobody wants to leave a desktop encoding around the clock for a 24/7 channel.
  • The setup is too much. Scenes, sources, canvas resolution, rescale output, keyframe interval, x264 presets, rate control — a lot of configuration to press play on an MP4.

If your goal is broadcasting a recorded video, every one of those problems disappears when the encoding moves off your machine.

What OBS actually does — and what replaces each part

OBS bundles four jobs into one program. Understanding the split makes it obvious which ones you still need:

OBS does thisDo you need it for a recorded video?
Captures a camera, screen or window in real timeNo — your video is already recorded.
Composites scenes, overlays and transitionsNo — that belongs in your video editor, where you have more control anyway.
Encodes to H.264/AACYes, but your export is already encoded. Streamza passes it through.
Pushes the result over RTMP to the platformYes — this is the part Streamza does for you, from the cloud.

So for pre-recorded content, OBS is doing one useful job: opening an RTMP connection and pumping frames into it. That job does not need to happen on your hardware.

How to go live with no software

  1. Copy your stream key

    From YouTube Studio, Twitch Creator Dashboard, Facebook Live Producer or your platform's equivalent. The stream key guide lists them all.

  2. Open Web Studio in any browser

    Go to streamza.live/studio on a laptop, a tablet or a phone and claim a free live slot. No install, no account, no card.

  3. Upload the video and paste the key

    Drop in your MP4. The RTMP URL is pre-filled for YouTube, Twitch and Facebook — for anything else, paste the server URL too.

  4. Press go, then close the tab

    The stream runs on our servers. Your browser is only the remote control, and you can shut it the moment the broadcast is live.

Frequently asked questions

Can I really live stream with no software installed?

Yes. Everything happens in the browser and on our servers. There is no download, no plugin and no extension — and because there is no local encoder, it works identically on Windows, macOS, Linux, Android and iOS.

Can I go live from my phone without an app?

Yes. Open streamza.live/studio in your mobile browser, upload a video from your camera roll, paste your key and start. There is also a separate Streamza Android app if you want live camera and screen streaming, which the web service does not do.

Is the quality worse than OBS?

Generally better, because there is no second encode. OBS re-encodes your file in real time and quality depends on your CPU keeping up. Streamza relays your existing export, so the resolution, frame rate and bitrate you chose in your editor are what reaches the platform.

What about Streamlabs, vMix, Wirecast or Restream Studio?

Same reasoning applies. They are all local encoders or live production suites built around real-time sources. For pushing a finished video file to an RTMP endpoint, none of that machinery is needed.

Can I stream my screen or webcam this way?

Not through the web service — it broadcasts video files. For live camera and screen capture, use OBS on a desktop or the Streamza Android app on a phone.

Does it work with any platform OBS works with?

Yes. Anything that accepts an RTMP or RTMPS ingest URL and a stream key works, which is every platform OBS can reach. See custom RTMP streaming.
