Why music radio is the original 24/7 stream format

Long before "always-on" streaming had a name, lofi hip hop and chillhop channels were already running it: one continuous mix, a slow-panning illustration, and a live badge that never turns off. It works because a live channel is discoverable in a way an uploaded playlist is not: it shows up in YouTube's live sections, it's there at 2 a.m. for someone who wants study music, and every new viewer arrives to an active chat and a channel that feels alive.

The same format now covers far more than lofi: chillhop, jazz, piano, synthwave, ambient techno, focus/binaural mixes, even radio-station simulcasts. If it's music people put on in the background for hours, it's a candidate for a 24/7 channel.

Get the rights right before anything else

This is the one place a music radio stream can go wrong that has nothing to do with the streaming setup. Before you loop anything for hours a day, make sure you actually have the right to:

  • Your own productions: beats, mixes or compositions you made yourself.
  • Royalty-free or Creative Commons libraries that explicitly permit streaming/monetised use.
  • A licensed catalogue where your agreement covers 24/7 broadcast, not just personal listening.

Unlicensed commercial tracks will get muted, claimed, or strike the channel. And because a 24/7 stream is playing constantly, the exposure is far higher than a single upload. See our Acceptable Use Policy before you commit to a permanent channel.

How to set up a 24/7 music stream

  1. Produce or license your mix

    20 minutes to a few hours works well, long enough that the loop point isn't obvious to a regular listener. Export H.264 + AAC in MP4, under 1.5 GB.

  2. Design a loop-friendly visual

    A static illustration, a slow-panning scene, or an animated loop behind the title. Skip on-screen clocks or "now playing" text tied to a specific moment.

  3. Create the live event on your platform

    Use a persistent stream key so the same broadcast URL survives restarts. The stream key guide has the exact steps for YouTube, Twitch and Facebook.

  4. Upload to Web Studio, enable loop, go live

    Claim a slot at streamza.live/studio, upload the file, paste your key, and turn on loop forever. Confirm the broadcast on the platform side, then close everything. The stream keeps running from the cloud.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need the rights to the music I stream 24/7?

Yes. Streaming copyrighted music you don't have a license for will get the stream muted or claimed, and repeated claims risk the channel. Use your own productions, royalty-free libraries, or music you hold a streaming license for. See our Acceptable Use Policy.

Does my computer need to stay on for a 24/7 lofi stream?

No. Once you claim a slot in Streamza Web Studio and go live, the file loops from our servers. You can close the tab and turn your computer off. The broadcast keeps running.

How long should a lofi mix be before it repeats?

Longer than you'd think. A 20-60 minute mix is a common sweet spot: long enough that regular listeners rarely notice the loop point, short enough to stay under the 1.5 GB upload limit at good quality.

Can I stream to YouTube and Twitch at the same time?

Yes, on the Multistream plan you can send the same looping mix to up to 3 platforms at once from a single upload.

Will YouTube demonetise a looping music stream?

Looping itself isn't the problem: platforms act on content with no added value or unlicensed music, not on the format. A well-produced mix with your own or licensed music, a visual, and a real channel around it is treated like any other live stream.

What visual should I use behind a music stream?

Anything loop-friendly: a static illustration, a slow pan, an animated loop, or simple lyric/track-title cards. Avoid on-screen clocks or dated text that gives away the restart.
