Before you start: YouTube's requirements

YouTube gates live streaming behind a few checks. Get these done first or the Stream tab will not give you a key:

  • Verify your channel with a phone number. Unverified channels cannot stream at all.
  • Enable live streaming and wait. First activation can take up to 24 hours, so do not leave this until an hour before your broadcast.
  • No live-streaming restrictions in the past 90 days. A previous strike locks you out for the rest of that window.
  • No subscriber minimum for encoder streaming. This is the one people get wrong. The 50-subscriber rule applies to going live from the YouTube mobile app. Streaming through an encoder, which is what Streamza does, has no subscriber requirement, so a brand-new channel can go live today.

How to stream a recorded video to YouTube

  1. Create the stream in YouTube Studio

    Go to YouTube Studio → Create → Go Live → Stream. Set the title, description, thumbnail, category and visibility. This is a normal live event; you are just going to feed it a file instead of a camera.

  2. Copy the stream key

    On the Stream tab, click Copy next to the stream key. Choose a persistent key if you plan to restart the broadcast later. A persistent key reuses the same URL and settings every time.

  3. Upload your video to Web Studio

    Open streamza.live/studio, claim a free slot, and upload your MP4. Select YouTube so the ingest URL fills itself in, then paste the key.

  4. Start sending, then click Go Live

    Press start in Streamza. Within a few seconds YouTube's preview shows your video and stream health turns green. Now click Go Live in YouTube Studio to make it public. Close the tab. Streamza keeps sending.

Order matters. YouTube will not let you click "Go Live" until it is actually receiving video. If the button is greyed out, the stream has not reached YouTube yet. Wait a few seconds, and check that the key was pasted with no trailing space.

Running a 24/7 YouTube channel

YouTube is the natural home for always-on looping channels: music, study radio, ambience, information boards. Three things make the difference between one that survives and one that dies quietly:

  • Use a persistent stream key. It keeps the same video URL and watch page across restarts, so links, embeds and search results do not break every time the stream reconnects.
  • Own everything in the loop. Content ID scans live streams. Background music you did not license will get the broadcast muted, blocked or claimed within minutes, and repeated hits put the channel's live privileges at risk.
  • Give it real substance. YouTube acts against channels it judges to be repetitive with nothing added. An original mix with your own visuals is fine; a slideshow of other people's images is not.

Troubleshooting

"Go Live" is greyed out

YouTube is not receiving video yet. Confirm the stream is running in Web Studio, re-copy the key, and check you did not paste the ingest URL into the key field by mistake.

Stream health says "not receiving enough data"

Usually a bitrate mismatch: your export is far above what YouTube expects for its resolution. Re-export within the recommended range above.

The stream is live but silent

The audio track is probably not AAC, or the file has no audio stream at all. Re-export as H.264 video with AAC audio.

It went live, then YouTube muted or blocked it

Content ID matched music or footage in the file. You need rights to everything you broadcast. See our DMCA policy.

The key stopped working

Single-use keys expire once the event ends. Generate a new one, or switch to a persistent key so it stays valid.

Frequently asked questions

Can I stream a pre-recorded video on YouTube Live?

Yes. YouTube accepts any RTMP feed at its live ingest, and it makes no distinction between frames from a camera and frames from a file. Broadcasting your own recorded video is a normal, supported use of YouTube Live.

Do I need 50 subscribers to go live?

Not for this. The 50-subscriber threshold applies to streaming from the YouTube mobile app. Streaming through an encoder or a service like Streamza has no subscriber minimum. You only need a phone-verified channel with live streaming enabled.

Will the stream be saved on my channel afterwards?

Yes. YouTube archives completed live streams to your channel as a video, so the broadcast keeps earning views after it ends.

Can I run a 24/7 YouTube live stream this way?

Yes. Enable looping and the video repeats continuously inside one unbroken connection. Use a persistent stream key so the watch page URL survives restarts.

Does my PC need to stay on?

No. Once the stream has started it runs entirely on Streamza's servers. Close the browser, shut the laptop, turn off your phone. The broadcast continues.

Where exactly is the YouTube stream key?

YouTube Studio → Create → Go Live → the Stream tab → the "Stream key" box. Full instructions for every platform are in the stream key guide.

Can I stream to YouTube and other platforms at the same time?

Yes, up to three destinations at once on the Multistream plan: YouTube plus Twitch plus Facebook, for example, from a single upload. See multistreaming.
