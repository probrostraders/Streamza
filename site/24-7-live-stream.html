What a 24/7 live stream actually is

A 24/7 stream is a live broadcast that never stops. Instead of a camera feed, it sends one video file — or a long mix — to the platform on repeat, so the channel is permanently live. This is the format behind every "lofi hip hop radio" channel, ambient fireplace stream, aquarium cam, radio station simulcast, crypto ticker board and looping product showcase you have scrolled past.

The appeal is simple: a live channel is discoverable in a way a video upload is not. It sits in the platform's live sections, it can be found at any hour by someone searching for background music or a study stream, and every viewer who arrives sees a live badge and an active chat. The channel works while you do not.

The problem with doing it on your own machine

The traditional approach is OBS on a spare computer, looping a media source, left running forever. It works right up until it doesn't:

  • Anything that touches the machine kills the stream. A Windows update, a driver crash, a power cut, someone unplugging it to vacuum.
  • It occupies a whole computer. Permanently. You cannot use it for anything demanding without risking dropped frames.
  • It burns electricity continuously. A desktop encoding video 24 hours a day, every day, for months.
  • It saturates your upload. A 6 Mbps outbound stream running non-stop makes everyone else in the house miserable.
  • Your ISP is now a single point of failure. Every brief outage becomes a dead channel and a lost live position.

Moving the loop into the cloud removes all five at once. The file lives on our servers, the encoding happens on our servers, and the RTMP connection to YouTube or Twitch originates from a datacentre rather than from your living room.

How to set up a 24/7 looping stream

  1. Export a loop-friendly video

    Anything from a few minutes to a full-length mix works. Keep it under 1.5 GB and export H.264 + AAC in MP4. See the loop tips below for making the seam invisible.

  2. Create the live event on your platform

    On YouTube, use a persistent stream key so the same URL survives restarts. Copy the RTMP URL and key — the stream key guide has the exact path for each platform.

  3. Upload to Web Studio and enable loop

    Claim a slot at streamza.live/studio, upload the file, paste your key, and turn on loop forever. This is the setting that makes it a 24/7 channel rather than a one-shot broadcast.

  4. Go live and walk away

    Start the stream, confirm the broadcast on the platform side, then close everything. Renew the slot before it expires and the channel simply keeps running.

Choosing a video that loops cleanly

The difference between an amateur 24/7 channel and one people leave on for hours is the seam — the moment the video restarts. A few things make it invisible:

  • Match the first and last frame. If the visual is a static scene or a slow animation, make the end state identical to the start state so the cut is imperceptible.
  • Fade audio at both ends, or better, cut on a beat so the music restarts in tempo. An abrupt audio jump is far more noticeable than a visual one.
  • Go longer than you think. A 3-minute loop repeats 480 times a day and viewers notice. Twenty minutes to two hours is a much better range.
  • Avoid on-screen clocks, dates or "welcome" text that gives away the restart.
  • Keep the frame rate constant. Variable frame rate exports, which phones produce by default, can drift over long broadcasts. Re-export as constant frame rate.

On looping and platform rules: looping your own content is allowed, but platforms do act against channels that are purely repetitive with no added value, and Facebook in particular limits how long a single broadcast can run. Check the platform guide for wherever you are streaming before committing to a permanent channel.

Frequently asked questions

Does my computer need to stay on for a 24/7 stream?

No. The video is stored and broadcast from Streamza's servers. After you press start you can close the tab and shut the machine down — the stream is no longer connected to your device in any way.

Is there a gap when the video loops?

No. Looping happens inside the same continuous RTMP connection, so the platform never sees the stream stop and start. Viewers see one unbroken broadcast; there is no reconnect and no dropped live position.

How long can one video be?

Up to 1.5 GB, which at typical 1080p bitrates is roughly 30–60 minutes of high quality video, or several hours for simpler visuals like a static image with music.

Will a 24/7 stream get me strikes or demonetised?

Not for looping itself, as long as you own the content. Streams get in trouble for using music or footage the channel does not have rights to, or for content platforms judge to be purely repetitive with no value added. Use your own material and you are on solid ground — see our Acceptable Use Policy.

Can I change the video without ending the stream?

No — swapping the source means stopping and restarting the broadcast. Plan the loop content before you go live, and build in enough variety that you will not want to change it for a while.

Can I run a 24/7 stream on several platforms at once?

Yes, up to three destinations at the same time on the Multistream plan. One looping upload feeds all of them — see multistreaming.

What happens if the platform disconnects the stream?

Platforms occasionally reset ingest connections during maintenance. Check on a long-running channel every so often, and if the platform has dropped the connection, restart the stream from Web Studio.
